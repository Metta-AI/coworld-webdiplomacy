# Brief: The League Desk — a local dashboard for running the webDiplomacy leagues

Doc type: Design proposal. Date: 2026-10-12. Status: Proposal, nothing built. Author: James Boggs with Claude.
Keep the document CONCISE: James asked for a short design doc. Target about 1,400 to 1,800 words of prose plus the
tables and two or three small figures. Do not pad.

A separate mock-up of the page exists next to this document: `league-dashboard-mockup-2026-10-12.html` (same folder).
The design doc must link to it near the top (relative link) and say that every number in the mock-up is invented.

## 1. Problem

James runs two webDiplomacy leagues on the Softmax platform: the press league (Game of the Week, `classic-press`) and
the gunboat league. Today the state of the league is spread over a dozen CLI commands and team-only API routes. On
2026-10-12 alone he changed the round interval (150 to 60 minutes), the daily budget ($15 to $100) and the game itself
(Coworld 0.7.10, 3-minute phases), and there is no single place to see what those changes did. He asked for a
single-page dashboard that runs locally and reads the Softmax API.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

Goals:
- One page, run locally, that answers in order: does anything need action now; who is competing and how sure are the
  ratings; is the league keeping its schedule; what does it cost; how are players using LLMs and experience requests;
  who beats whom; what happens on the board.
- Every panel states its finding in a sentence above the chart, and names where its data comes from.
- Read-only. The dashboard never changes league settings.

Non-goals:
- Not a public page. It shows other users' private experience requests, email addresses and spend.
- Not a replacement for the Observatory site or for the optimizer IDE (which is one player's view of one policy).
- Not real-time. Rounds take about an hour; a refresh every minute or two is enough.

## 3. The page (see the mock-up)

Name: "The League Desk". One dense page, 12-column grid, Softmax "Ink & Print" house style (cream paper, ink, quiet
hairline panels, no decorative cards). Controls in the masthead: League (Press | Gunboat), Range (24 h | 7 d | 30 d |
All), Coworld version (All | 0.7.10 | 0.7.9), Refresh. Every time chart marks Coworld version changes, because a
version can change game length and therefore ratings, durations and cost.

Sections, top to bottom, with the panels in each:

1. **Now** (a ruled strip, not tiles): current round and when it is due; next round slot; budget today with a meter
   and a projection to the reset; canonical Coworld version; games, retries and failures in the last 24 h. Beside it a
   "Needs attention" list: a policy one failure from disqualification, a model whose calls fail, a seat hitting its
   LLM cap, mixed-version data.
2. **Standings and rating**: one table row per competing player with policy version, model, MMR, the rating interval
   (mu ± 3 sigma) drawn on one shared scale, a 20-round sparkline, 24 h change, games, mean score against par,
   submissions in 24 h and 7 d, LLM spend per game. Then MMR over time (top three labelled, the rest grey),
   submissions per day, and membership and exposure (active, benched, disqualified, filler share of seats, time from
   submission to first league game, games per player per round).
3. **Rounds, games and time**: round duration against the round interval; game duration (median and p90); games per
   day and how games ended; and one full-width bar showing where an episode's time goes (queue, pod start, boot,
   connect wait, movement phases, retreats and builds, adjudication, results and upload).
4. **Money and model use**: daily spend against budget (compute and LLM); league spend against experience-request
   spend; LLM spend per seat per game against the cap; models in use (policies, players, calls, failed calls, spend);
   cost per round and per game.
5. **Experience requests**: requests per day by requester; queue and play time by variant; common config overrides.
6. **Matchups and the board**: head-to-head grid by player; score by power for all players with intervals; most and
   least contested provinces; score by policy and power; phase timing; time to Ready per policy; seat health (phases
   with no orders, rejected orders, lost pods, early exits).

Design rules used: finding first, chart second; one y-axis per chart; a reference line wherever a limit exists
(round interval, budget, seat cap, par 0.143); cells with fewer than five games are drawn empty and dashed, never
coloured; colour follows the entity; status colours (terracotta for a problem) are not reused for series.

Additions beyond James's list (state these as proposals): the "Needs attention" list; version markers and a version
filter; league against experience-request spend; membership and exposure; filler share; time from submission to first
game; phase timing; seat health; a data-source tag on every panel.

## 5. What the API survey found (short section; these are facts read on 2026-10-12)

- All but two of the platform metrics have a route. The two gaps are per-round rating history and the LLM model a
  policy was uploaded with.
- A team-only read-only SQL route exists (`POST /sql/query`, 20-second timeout, at most 1,000 rows, cut off silently).
  It is the only way to get per-seat LLM spend in bulk and the stored request spec of experience requests.
- Experience requests cost far more than the league. For the week read on 2026-10-12 the press league's tournament
  spend was about $10.83 and experience-request spend on the same Coworld was about $1,422. The daily league budget
  ($100) counts tournament spend only. This is why the page has a league-against-experience-requests panel.
- Only rounds 7 to 9 are visible in the press league: earlier rounds were hidden when rounds were reset on 2026-10-12.
  History charts start there.
- Rate limits are generous for one user: 1,500 requests a minute and 12,500 an hour. The admin cost and usage routes
  are unpaginated scans, so poll them slowly.
- Experience-request rows carry the requester's email address.
- Per-episode `cost_usd` is compute only. LLM spend is attributed separately, and the round cost route reported
  $0.00 LLM for a round whose league shows LLM spend for the week. Treat per-round LLM cost as unverified.

## 6. Architecture

Three parts, all local, all synchronous Python:

1. **Collector.** Polls the API with James's own login and the elevated header, and writes rows to a local SQLite
   file. Incremental: it asks only for rounds, episodes and requests newer than what it has. It also takes the rating
   snapshot after each completed round, and downloads `results.json` and the replay of each finished league game
   (the optimizer's `fetch_league.py` already does this download).
2. **Metrics.** SQL views and small Python functions over the SQLite file. Board metrics reuse the optimizer lab's
   loader and tools (`webdip_episodes.py`, `matchups.py`, `board_counts.py`, `batches.py`) instead of new parsers.
3. **Page.** One server-rendered page from a small FastAPI and Jinja app, the same stack the optimizer IDE in this
   repository already uses. Charts drawn with one charting library.

Suggested polling: league health, budget and the current round every 60 seconds; memberships, submissions,
leaderboard and experience requests every 5 minutes; cost and LLM usage scans every 15 minutes; artifacts once per
finished game.

Location: `ops/league-desk/` in this repository as its own uv project (like `optimizer/ide`). It is not part of the
optimizer template: players cannot call the team-only routes. The SQLite file and downloaded artifacts stay in an
ignored directory. The app prints no tokens and binds to localhost only.

artifacts (results.json, replay) ; SQLite → metrics → page. Keep it simple.

## 7. Decisions, with the alternatives that lost

Decision 1: a local cache (SQLite), not live queries per page load.
- Why: rating history does not exist on the platform, so something must store snapshots anyway; the cost routes are
  slow scans; artifacts must be downloaded once and parsed.
- Rejected: query the API on every page load. Slow, loses rating history, and repeats the cost scans.

Decision 2: FastAPI and Jinja, reusing the pattern of `optimizer/ide`.
- Why: it is already in this repository (same dependencies, same test style), it gives full control of a dense
  single page in the house style, and it stays synchronous.
- Rejected: Streamlit (most popular Python dashboard tool, Apache-2.0, released 2026-10-02). Fastest to a first
  version, but a dense 12-column page with custom table cells, shared-scale interval bars and the house style fights
  its layout model. It is the right choice if speed matters more than the single-page design.
- Rejected: Grafana (AGPL-3.0, very active). Strong for time series and alerts, weak for the standings table, the
  matchup grid and board charts, and it means running a server and a data-source plugin for a one-person tool.
- Rejected: Evidence (MIT, SQL and Markdown reports) and Observable Framework (ISC; last push 2026-05-15). Both are
  static-site builders that fit the data, but each adds a Node toolchain to a Python repository, and Observable
  Framework's activity has slowed.
- Rejected: marimo (Apache-2.0, active). A notebook, good for exploration, not for a fixed operations page.

Decision 3: one charting library, not hand-drawn SVG. Recommendation: Observable Plot (ISC), which draws SVG that the
page's CSS can style and handles dot-and-interval, cell and line marks directly. Its release pace is slow (latest
release 2025-02-14, last push 2026-09-01), so Apache ECharts is the fallback. The mock-up's charts are hand-drawn
only because it is a static mock-up.

Decision 4: read board metrics from each game's public files, not from new platform features.
- Why: `results.json` and the replay already hold scores by power, adjudicated orders and phase transitions.

## 8. Instrumentation the game needs (smallest first)

Render as a table: Change | Gives | Where | Privacy note.

1. Record connection events (connect, disconnect, reconnect per slot with elapsed time) | seat health | `adapter/routes.py` | none; slot-level only.
2. Read upstream's per-key API hit counter at the end of the game | API requests per seat | `php/wdc_state.php` | never log the key; join by user to slot.
3. Store each phase's deadline and the longest gamemaster request per phase | phases ended early; adjudication time | `adapter/episode.py` | none.
4. A `timing` object in `results.json` and one `{"event":"timing"}` line on stdout at exit | boot, game creation, connect wait, results writing, shutdown | `adapter/boot.py`, `adapter/episode.py` | none.
5. Sample each seat's order status every tick; record first Ready and first Saved per phase; write only when the game ends | time to Ready; phases never Ready; phases with no orders | `php/wdc_state.php`, `Episode.tick` | upstream hides this in anonymous games; it must never reach the live spectator stream, replay frames or stdout during play.
6. Private press message COUNTS per seat per phase | negotiation volume | end-of-game query | `results.json` goes to every player; counts reveal who talked to whom.

Any new `results.json` field also needs `docs/protocol.md`, the manifest sync and a new Coworld version (0.7.11).
None of these changes touches the upstream submodule or advances a phase.

## 9. Build order

1. API-only page: sections 1 to 5 of the page and the head-to-head grid. No game change needed.
2. Artifact ingest: score by power, contested provinces, end reasons, phase timing.
3. Coworld 0.7.11 with instrumentation items 1 to 5: timing profile, time to Ready, seat health.
4. Platform timing split from Datadog job traces, joined to the game's timing record.

## 10. Open questions (for James)

1. Should Ready timing and missed-order data, once recorded, go into `results.json` (which every player in the game
   receives) or into a team-only artifact? Publishing it after the game tells rivals how each bot behaved.
2. Same question for private press counts. Recommendation: per-seat totals only, or team-only.
3. Rating history: accept "history starts when the dashboard first runs", or also backfill by replaying OpenSkill
   (which may not match the platform's numbers exactly)?
4. The SQL route is an internal team tool and could change. Is it acceptable for per-seat spend and request
   overrides to depend on it, or should we ask the platform team for proper routes?
5. Experience-request spend is about 130 times league spend this week and has no budget. Is that intended? The
   dashboard will show it either way.
6. Will anyone besides James use this? It shows email addresses and private requests.
7. Charting library: Observable Plot or ECharts.
